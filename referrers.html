For the people who see the grease before anyone else.

Measured verification for commercial kitchen exhaust systems — Adelaide. Kitchen exhaust compliance certificates are commonly self-declared: a sticker and a sentence. Summit’s reports the measurements.

01  What the certificate shows

Every certificate,
without exception.

Grease-depth readings in microns

At every duct access point opened, before and after works — reported against the AIRAH Best Practice Guideline (Commercial Kitchen Exhaust Management) benchmark: mean grease depth below 0.2 mm (200 μm), no single reading above 2 mm (2,000 μm). Numbers, not adjectives.

Identical-position before-and-after photographs

At every point — comparable evidence, not two unrelated pictures.

A numbered deficiency register

Every deficiency observed, with location, severity (Critical, Major, Minor), photographic reference and recommended action.

Full-vs-partial disclosure

A plain statement of what was serviced, what was not, and why — before anyone has to ask.

Works performed and certified under AS 1851-2012, the current edition for routine service of fire protection systems and equipment.

02  Why it matters to your clients

Three trades.
One document.

INSURANCE BROKERS

Claims-ready evidence, on file before it is needed

Some insurers condition hospitality cover on documented kitchen exhaust servicing — and after a fire, the claims assessor asks six things: what was done, when, to what standard, measured how, by whom, and what was known to be outstanding. An invoice answers none of them. A Summit Certificate answers all six from the document alone: dated works, measured readings against a recognised industry benchmark, photographic evidence, and a deficiency register showing what was known and what was recommended.

For you, that is claims-ready evidence sitting on file, a cleaner conversation at renewal, and one less unknown in the risk being placed. For your client, it is the difference between asserting their maintenance and proving it.

FIRE-SUPPRESSION TECHNICIANS

Work flowing to your trade, in writing

You see the state of the exhaust system at every suppression service — and you see it before anyone else does. Its condition is also your working environment: nozzles, detection lines and fusible links sit in the same airstream as the grease.

Summit documents the whole system factually — measured readings, photographs, a numbered deficiency register — and where a survey records a suppression-side deficiency, it is documented for the venue and referred back to their suppression technician: work flowing to your trade, in writing, not around it. When a kitchen asks who does the ductwork, you have an answer whose paperwork you have already seen.

KITCHEN INSTALLERS & FOOD-SAFETY CONSULTANTS

A baseline your recommendation can stand on

A new canopy or a food-safety audit is the moment a venue's exhaust file gets opened. A measured baseline — readings, photographs, a register — gives your recommendation something to stand on, and gives the venue a record that starts on day one rather than at the first incident.

03  What a referral looks like

Simple and professional. The whole ask is an introduction.

You mention Summit where it genuinely fits. Summit delivers a free site survey to qualifying venues, and the venue keeps the written report whether or not they engage further. No scripts, no targets, no obligation in either direction — and you see the standard of the documentation before your name is ever near it.

The report never names, grades or characterises any current provider: it measures the system and writes down what it finds.

04  The free site survey

A working sample,
on their own kitchen.

For venues inside Summit's target profile, the first step is free: a 15–30 minute measured site survey — the same instruments, the same benchmark and the same evidence discipline as the certificate — producing a written condition report the venue keeps, engaged or not. Outside that profile, the same survey is available as a standalone condition-report engagement.

If you know a kitchen that should see one, that introduction is the whole ask.
