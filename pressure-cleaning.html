The surfaces that fail you first.

Bin rooms, grease pads, loading docks and entries — cleaned on a schedule, with the washwater contained and the condition recorded. Back of house, kept to the same standard as the front.

Why it matters

Nobody audits the dining room. They audit the bin room.

The surfaces that carry your risk are the ones nobody photographs: the grease pad under the exhaust discharge, the waste area, the loading dock your staff cross forty times a shift. They accumulate slowly, they fail an inspection quickly, and a slip on any one of them is a claim against you.

Summit treats them the way it treats every other asset — on a planned cycle, cleaned to a defined standard, with the condition photographed and filed alongside the rest of your compliance record. Not a callout when a health inspector has already been.

02  The water question

Where the washwater goes
is your liability.

Most pressure cleaning ends with grease, detergent and food waste running into the nearest stormwater grate. That water leaves your site as your discharge — and as the occupier, the exposure stays with you. It is the part of this trade nobody advertises, and the reason we scope it first.

Contained, not chased

Drains are protected and washwater is contained within the work area before the first trigger is pulled — containment is part of the setup, not a reaction.

Collected & removed

Contaminated water is recovered and taken off site for lawful disposal, or directed to trade waste where your agreement permits it.

Recorded like everything else

Method and disposal noted on the service record, so if anyone ever asks how the water was handled, the answer is already written down.

Scoped before we quote

Every site is assessed for drainage, fall and containment first. If a surface can't be cleaned without a discharge problem, we say so before the job, not after.
