Compliance guide · South Australia

To what standard, with what proof, by when.

The three questions every operator and building owner eventually asks about kitchen exhaust compliance — answered plainly for South Australia.

01  The standard

AS 1851-2012. Canopy to discharge.

AS 1851-2012, routine service of fire protection systems and equipment, is the maintenance regime behind most fire-safety line items on an Australian building. Its Section 13 covers the fire and smoke control features of mechanical services, which is where a commercial kitchen's exhaust system lives: the canopy and filters, the plenum, the ductwork and riser, the fan and the discharge.

Grease accumulating in that run is a fire load inside the building fabric — which is why it is a fire-safety maintenance item, not a housekeeping one. The current edition is 2012. The standard’s tables are cited, never reproduced.

02  What sets your interval

Your kitchen sets the clock.
The reading confirms it.

Your interval is set at the site survey from cooking load, fuel type, hours and the measured grease accumulation between services, and is written on your certificate.

Cooking load

Covers per service, the equipment under the canopy, and how hard it runs.

Fuel type

Solid fuel, char grills and woks load a system faster than gas or electric equipment.

Hours of operation

A kitchen trading two services a day accumulates differently from one trading a lunch.

Measured accumulation

The reading between services is what actually decides the interval — it confirms or corrects the first three.

Where the standard's own servicing frequency for a duty class is discussed, Summit does not assert a number on its behalf. The full explanation is in how often a commercial kitchen exhaust needs cleaning.

03  The evidence bar

A schedule without records
is a promise, not compliance.

Whoever services your system, hold them to the same evidence bar. A defensible record shows:

01A certificate that names AS 1851-2012 — not “industry standard”.
02Grease-depth readings in microns, before and after, against a stated benchmark.
03Photographs of each position, before and after, from the identical position.
04Full-vs-partial disclosure, a numbered deficiency register, and the next service set.

04  South Australia

In SA, the record feeds the owner's Form 3.

Each year the building owner lodges the Essential Safety Provisions maintenance verification within 60 business days of the calendar year's end. Where the kitchen exhaust system is on the ESP schedule, that verification relies on maintenance evidence — the readings, photographs and register described above.

05  Common questions

Asked by every operator.
Answered plainly.

How often does a commercial kitchen exhaust system need cleaning?

There is no honest one-word answer. Your interval is set at the site survey from cooking load, fuel type, hours of operation and the measured grease accumulation between services, and it is written on your certificate. Summit does not assert a servicing frequency on the standard's behalf; the reading decides.

What must a kitchen exhaust compliance certificate contain?

The standard named — AS 1851-2012 — readings in microns at every access point against a stated benchmark, identical-position before-and-after photographs, a full-vs-partial statement, a numbered deficiency register, the next service, and a certificate number that can be traced. A certificate that cannot show its readings is a sticker with a sentence.

Will a generic certificate satisfy my insurer?

Often it is not accepted at face value. After a kitchen fire, insurers request maintenance records and look for a dated evidence chain: the standard named, measured readings, photographs, and what was known to be outstanding. A certificate that asserts a result without evidencing it invites dispute at the moment you can least afford one.

Do clean filters mean the system is compliant?

Filters catch grease at the canopy and load far faster than the system behind them. A full system service covers the canopy, plenum, ductwork, riser and fan. A serviced filter set alone does not make a compliant system, and a certified duct service does not excuse a loaded filter bank — the two load at different rates and are measured separately, with the canopy and filters recorded as their own line on the certificate.

Do you service the whole duct run or just what is reachable?

The scope is the whole system. Where a section cannot be accessed, the certificate says so — full-vs-partial disclosure — with the reason and a numbered register item recommending the remedy, typically an access panel. An undeclared gap becomes your problem at audit; a declared one gets fixed.

What is Form 3?

South Australia's Essential Safety Provisions maintenance verification. The building owner lodges it with council within 60 business days of the end of the calendar year, confirming the building's listed provisions have been maintained. Where the kitchen exhaust system sits on the ESP schedule, that confirmation relies on maintenance evidence.

Is the site survey really free, and for whom?

It is free for hotel groups, aged care, centre food courts, venue operators and facilities-managed sites in metro Adelaide, from a single venue up. Outside that profile, the same survey is available as a paid condition report. Either way the venue keeps the written report, whether or not it engages Summit.

You are new. Why trust the certificate?

Because it is verifiable before you engage. The certificate specification is published on this site, the survey produces a working sample on your own kitchen, and the written method is available on request. The evidence is on the page; it does not ask for trust.

Do you assess my current provider?

No. The survey measures the asset and writes down what it finds. No provider is named, graded or characterised in any Summit document. The readings and photographs speak for the system.

Do we need to close the kitchen?

Servicing is planned around the operation — typically outside trading hours, scheduled ahead on the program — so the kitchen opens as normal.

How does this work across a multi-site group?

One schedule, one invoice, consolidated reporting. Programs are shaped by venue count — 1, 2 to 5, or 6 and more — and each site keeps its own register and certificate history, rolled up for the group.
