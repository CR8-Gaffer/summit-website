Why it matters

A clean filter is not a clean system. But a blocked one loads everything behind it.

Filters catch what they can; the rest goes on into the plenum, the duct and the fan. A filter that is blocked, bent or missing lets more through, pulls harder on the fan and raises the fire load in the canopy. It is also the one part of the system a health or fire inspector can see from the floor.

Summit treats the filters as a line on the record: counted and sized at the survey, exchanged on cycle, and photographed in place. Whether the system behind them is within criteria is a separate question, answered by the readings. Why clean filters do not make the system compliant

What is included

Exchange, replacement and the record.

Counted and sized at the survey

Before anything is quoted

Every canopy’s filters are counted, measured and photographed at the survey, so the exchange is scoped to the kitchen rather than estimated.

Exchanged on cycle

Clean set in, soiled set out

On each service visit the soiled filters come out and a clean set goes in, on the same calendar as the exhaust program, so the kitchen is never without a full set.

Replacements supplied to size

Damaged or missing

A bent frame, a missing baffle or a wrong-size filter is replaced with one supplied to the canopy’s measurement, and the replacement is noted on the record.

Recorded on the certificate

Count, size, condition

The canopy and filters are their own line on the certificate, with the count, the size and a photograph, so the next inspector can see what was there and when.

Who it is for

Any kitchen on a program, and a few that are not.

Kitchens on a Summit program

The usual route

Filter exchange is scoped at the same survey as the exhaust system and runs on the same visits. Most kitchens take it this way.

Groups and multi-site operators

One schedule, one count

Filter counts and sizes held per site, exchanged on each site’s cycle, and summarised for head office with the rest of the program.

Kitchens that clean their own filters

Replacements only

Where the kitchen soaks its own filters between services, Summit supplies replacements to size when one is damaged or missing, and records the set at each visit.

How it runs

Survey to certificate, on one cycle.

STAGE 01

Count

Filters counted, measured and photographed at the survey; the cycle set to the kitchen’s load.

STAGE 02

Exchange

Soiled set out, clean set in, on each scheduled visit.

STAGE 03

Replace

Any damaged or missing filter replaced to size and noted on the record.

STAGE 04

Record

Count, size, condition and photograph written on the certificate with the rest of the system.

Start

Put the filters on the record.

The survey counts and sizes them; the program keeps them clean and on the certificate.
